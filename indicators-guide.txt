=====================================================================
 GUIDE TO THE 5 INDICATORS IN YOUR PRICE TRACKER
=====================================================================

IMPORTANT FIRST
- This is an explanation of how to read charts. It is NOT financial
  advice, and I am not a financial advisor.
- No indicator predicts prices. Each one describes what the price HAS
  been doing, and traders use that as a hint about what MAY happen.
- Indicators are often wrong. Never rely on one alone, and never risk
  money you cannot afford to lose.

All indicators in your app use ONE PRICE PER HOUR, in USD, taken from
the prices your tracker saved. "Hours" in the settings means hourly
points (10 hours = the last 10 hourly prices).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 1. TWO MOVING AVERAGES  (a "fast" line and a "slow" line)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
A moving average (MA) is the average price over the last N hours. It
smooths the small ups and downs so you can see the direction.
  - FAST average (short, e.g. 20 hours): follows the price closely.
  - SLOW average (long, e.g. 50 hours): shows the bigger trend.
  - SMA = simple average. EMA = gives more weight to recent prices, so
    it reacts faster.

HOW TO READ IT
  - Price above both lines, lines rising      -> uptrend.
  - Price below both lines, lines falling     -> downtrend.
  - Fast line crosses ABOVE the slow line     -> green triangle on the
    chart. Traders call it a possible BUY signal ("the trend may be
    turning up").
  - Fast line crosses BELOW the slow line     -> red triangle. A
    possible SELL signal ("the trend may be turning down").
  - Lines tangled together and crossing often -> the price is moving
    sideways. The signals are mostly noise then.

LIMITS
  - It LAGS: the signal arrives after the move has already started.
  - In a sideways market it gives many false signals.

SETTINGS IN THE APP
  Average type (Simple / Exponential), Fast hours, Slow hours.
  The fast value should be smaller than the slow one.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. RSI  (Relative Strength Index)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
A number between 0 and 100 that measures how fast and how strongly
the price has risen or fallen recently.

HOW TO READ IT
  - Above 70  -> the price rose fast. It may be "stretched" (overbought)
                 and could pull back. It is NOT an automatic sell.
  - Below 30  -> the price fell fast. It may be "oversold" and could
                 bounce. It is NOT an automatic buy.
  - Around 50 -> balanced, no strong push either way.
  - In a strong uptrend RSI can stay above 70 for a long time. In a
    strong downtrend it can stay below 30. So use it with the trend.

LIMITS
  - It works best when the price moves up and down in a range. In a
    strong trend, its warnings come too early.

SETTINGS IN THE APP
  RSI hours (7 = faster and jumpier, 14 = standard, 21 = smoother).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 3. MACD  (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
It compares two exponential averages (12 hours and 26 hours). The
difference is the MACD line. A third, smoother line (9 hours) is the
SIGNAL line. The bars show the gap between MACD and the signal line.
(These 12 / 26 / 9 values are the standard ones and are fixed.)

HOW TO READ IT
  - MACD line crosses ABOVE the signal line -> momentum turning up
    (green bars appear above the zero line).
  - MACD line crosses BELOW the signal line -> momentum turning down
    (red bars appear below the zero line).
  - Bars getting SHORTER means the push is weakening, even if the price
    is still moving the same way.
  - MACD above zero means the short-term trend is above the long-term
    trend. Below zero means the opposite.

LIMITS
  - Like the moving averages, it lags. It gives many false crosses when
    the price moves sideways.

SETTINGS IN THE APP
  None (fixed at 12 / 26 / 9).


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 4. BOLLINGER BANDS
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
A middle line (a simple moving average) with an upper and a lower band
around it. The bands are placed a number of "standard deviations" away,
which simply means: the more the price swings, the wider the bands.

HOW TO READ IT
  - Price near the UPPER band   -> stretched up. Could cool off.
  - Price near the LOWER band   -> stretched down. Could bounce.
  - Price riding along the upper band in a strong trend is normal.
    Touching a band is not a signal by itself.
  - Bands NARROW (squeeze)      -> the market is quiet. A bigger move
    often follows, but the bands do not tell you which direction.
  - Bands WIDE                  -> the market is volatile. Use smaller
    positions or wider stop-losses.

LIMITS
  - A touch of the band is not a reason to buy or sell. Price can walk
    along a band for a long time.

SETTINGS IN THE APP
  Bands hours (10 / 20 / 50) and Width (1.5 / 2 / 2.5). Wider = fewer
  touches. The standard setting is 20 hours and width 2.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 5. BACKTEST
---------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT IS
It replays a simple rule on your saved price history and shows what
WOULD have happened. The chart starts at 100 at the beginning of the
selected period. The orange line is the rule. The grey line is simply
holding the asset the whole time.

THE RULES YOU CAN TEST
  - Fast average above slow average : in the asset while the fast line
                                      is above the slow line, in cash
                                      otherwise.
  - RSI                             : buy when RSI < 30, sell when > 70.
  - MACD                            : in the asset while MACD is above
                                      its signal line.
  - Bollinger Bands                 : buy below the lower band, sell when
                                      the price rises above the middle.

WHAT THE NUMBERS MEAN
  - Closed trades : how many buy-then-sell rounds were completed.
  - Winning       : the share of closed trades that made money.
  - Rule result   : total gain or loss of the rule in the period.
  - Just holding  : gain or loss if you had simply kept the asset.
  - Worst drop    : the biggest fall from a high point of the orange
                    line. It tells you how much pain you would have
                    had to sit through.
  - Cost per trade: a fee or spread (in %) taken at each buy and sell.
                    Keep it above 0, otherwise results look too good.

HOW TO JUDGE IT
  - A rule is only interesting if it beats "just holding" AFTER costs,
    with a worst drop you could live with.
  - Fewer than about 10 closed trades means the result is luck, not
    proof. The app shows a warning. Use Month / Year / All and load
    past prices to get more history.
  - The backtest looks at the past only. A rule that worked before can
    fail tomorrow.
  - Do NOT keep changing the settings until the result looks good. That
    is called overfitting: it fits the past perfectly and fails on new
    data. Choose sensible settings first and test them once.
  - It assumes you could buy and sell at the saved hourly price, with
    no delays. Real trading is a little worse.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 HOW TO USE THEM TOGETHER (a simple routine)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. TREND: use the two moving averages. Is the price above or below the
   slow average? Trade with that direction rather than against it.
2. TIMING: look at RSI and MACD. Do they agree with the trend? For
   example, in an uptrend, an RSI dip toward 30-40 or a MACD turning up
   again can mark a better entry than buying after a big jump.
3. RISK: look at the Bollinger Bands. Wide bands = big swings, so use a
   smaller position and a wider stop-loss.
4. TEST: before trusting any rule, check the Backtest over a long
   period (Month / Year / All).
5. ALERT: set a price alert or a "fast crosses slow" alert so the page
   tells you when something changes (only while the page is open).

Do not stack many indicators that say the same thing. MACD and the
moving averages, for example, are both built from averages, so they
often agree. Using one for trend, one for momentum (RSI) and one for
volatility (Bollinger Bands) gives more different information.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 THINGS NO INDICATOR CAN SEE
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- Central bank interest-rate decisions (US Fed, ECB, Bank of England,
  Bank of Japan, Swiss National Bank).
- Inflation and jobs reports, especially from the US.
- The overall strength of the US dollar.
- Wars, crises and sudden news. Gold and the Swiss franc often rise
  when investors are afraid.
Big announcements can move prices much more than any indicator
suggests. Many traders reduce or close positions before them.


---------------------------------------------------------------------
 LIMITS OF YOUR TRACKER'S DATA
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- Prices come from free sources and can be delayed.
- Currency prices on the live page update slowly, so do not use them
  for very fast trading.
- Hourly points mean the indicators react to slow moves, not to
  minute-by-minute ones.
- The history is only as long as you have saved or loaded. Short
  history makes every indicator less reliable.

Good habits matter more than any signal: decide in advance how much
you can afford to lose, where you will exit if you are wrong, and keep
each trade small compared to your total money.
